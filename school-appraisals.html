Read what the school sent. Before your visit the school self-rates all 31 aspects and attaches evidence to each. Open a record and go domain by domain — each aspect shows their rating, their documents, and the framework's description of the band they claimed.
2
Rate it yourself, per visit. Each school is visited twice — Phase 1 for teaching quality, Phase 3 to validate. Use the phase switcher to pick which visit you are recording. Where you differ from the school, you must say why; the save is blocked until you do.
3
Write the report. The Report tab drafts itself from your ratings, the differences against the school's, and your reasons for them. Edit it, compose it into one document, format it, then publish. Publishing is what the school sees.
4
Follow the action plan. The school has four weeks after publication to answer with an action per recommendation. You cannot edit their plan — your part is a dated progress review, which the mid-year check-in expects.
5
Handle any appeal. A school may challenge a domain rating within 10 working days of the report. Appeals appear on the record; a Central Hub admin decides each one with the domain lead, within 15 working days, and that decision is final.

Academic Basis · School Appraisal Framework v2.0

Why our school appraisal is structured this way

Eduversal's School Appraisal Framework is the international + Indonesia cross-walk of the most established institutional self-evaluation models in the world. Five domains (Leadership & Governance · Teaching & Learning Quality · Curriculum · Student Achievement · Culture & Community), 4-band scoring (Beginning / Developing / Established / Exemplary), school self-rating + Eduversal coordinator validation — the architecture used by CIS, WASC, NEASC, and Indonesia's own BAN-S/M accreditation system, with Cambridge International school-quality guidance and Permendiknas No. 13/2007 principal competency standards layered in.

🌍 Global
CIS International Accreditation
Council of International Schools · 11th Edition, 2024

8 protocol domains (Purpose & Direction · Governance & Leadership · The Curriculum · Teaching & Assessing for Learning · Student Wellbeing · Staffing · Premises & Resources · Community Partnerships). Our 5-domain split + validation visit cycle directly mirrors CIS school self-study + peer review.

🇺🇸 US
WASC Accrediting Commission for Schools
Western Association of Schools and Colleges · ACS WASC Postsecondary

Self-study Categories A (Organization) · B (Student Learning) · C (Culture) · D (Curriculum/Instruction/Assessment) · E (Improvement). Each of our 5 domains maps to one of these categories. Self-study + visiting committee validation is the WASC operating model we adopt.

🇺🇸 US
NEASC ACE Learning Standards
New England Association of Schools and Colleges · 2022 revision

8 learning principles + 4 foundation standards. Pioneered the "Learning Focus" self-evaluation model — schools rate against learner-centred outcomes rather than just compliance. Our Domain 4 (Student Achievement & Learning Outcomes) carries this DNA.

🎓 Cambridge
Cambridge School Quality + School Leader Standards
Cambridge International · CSLS 2023 (25 standards × 5 areas) + School Quality Programme Guidance 2024

Cambridge's own self-evaluation framework + the 25 School Leader Standards we tag against every domain (see the CSLS chips in the domain headers). The internal QA layer for partner schools running Cambridge programmes.

🇮🇩 ID
BAN-S/M Akreditasi · IASP 2020
Badan Akreditasi Nasional Sekolah/Madrasah · Instrumen Akreditasi Satuan Pendidikan

Four components — Mutu Lulusan · Proses Pembelajaran · Mutu Guru · Manajemen Sekolah. The legal Indonesian school accreditation framework. Our domains 2/3/4 satisfy the IASP rubric, and the Eduversal coordinator validation visits double as IASP-readiness audits.

🇮🇩 ID
Standar Kepala Sekolah/Madrasah
Permendiknas No. 13/2007 + Permendikbudristek successors

5 principal competencies — Kepribadian · Manajerial · Kewirausahaan · Supervisi · Sosial. The legal Indonesian principal competency baseline. Our D1 (Leadership & Governance) + D5 (Culture & Community) carry PMD tags to these 5 pillars so principal appraisal cross-walks with school appraisal.

Why self-appraisal first, then external validation: Decades of school-improvement research show that schools improve when they own the self-evaluation cycle and external validators play a "critical friend" rather than inspector role. Self-rating + commendations + recommendations is the CIS/WASC/NEASC model; the "Eduversal coordinator validation visit" is the critical-friend layer adapted from Robinson's Best Evidence Synthesis + Hallinger & Heck on instructional leadership impact pathways. — Robinson, V. 📚 Academic literature backing institutional self-appraisal
  • Robinson, V. (2009). School Leadership and Student Outcomes: Identifying What Works and Why. NZ Ministry of Education Best Evidence Synthesis — the single highest-leverage school-leadership evidence synthesis to date.
  • Hallinger, P., & Heck, R. H. (2010). Collaborative leadership and school improvement: Understanding the impact on school capacity and student learning. School Effectiveness and School Improvement, 21(2) — instructional leadership → school capacity → student outcomes pathway.
  • OECD (2013). Synergies for Better Learning: An International Perspective on Evaluation and Assessment. Five-layer evaluation architecture (system · school · leader · teacher · student).
  • MacBeath, J. (2006). School Inspection and Self-Evaluation: Working with the New Relationship. Routledge — foundational critique of inspector-vs-self-evaluator role boundaries.
  • CIS (2024). International Accreditation Programme — 11th Edition Protocol. Council of International Schools — current self-study + peer-review operating model.
  • OECD (2024). TALIS 2024 Results, Vol. I–II. International evidence on school evaluation practices + leadership feedback culture.
  • Bryk, A. S. et al. (2010). Organizing Schools for Improvement: Lessons from Chicago. University of Chicago Press — empirical basis for community engagement (D5) as a school-improvement lever.
🏷️ How framework tags work in this appraisal

The School Self-Appraisal page (Academic Hub) renders framework-anchor chips on every domain header so a partner-school principal can trace which international + Indonesia standard each domain anchors to. Click any popover-enabled chip to open the verbatim source:

  • CSLS Cambridge School Leader Standards 2023 — 25 standards × 5 areas (leadership / culture / practices / improvement / community)
  • ES Eduversal Academic Standards — network-wide policy authority (section-level, e.g. ES 7 = Teaching Quality, ES 9 = Safeguarding)
  • PMD Permendiknas No. 16/2007 four-pillar competency (Pedagogik · Profesional · Sosial · Kepribadian + Manajerial / Kewirausahaan for principals)

The following framework names tag each domain (static reference, no popover yet):

  • CIS · WASC · NEASC — international accreditation bodies (current 2024 editions)
  • IASP-N · BAN-S/M — Indonesia legal accreditation framework
  • Permendiknas No. 13/2007 — Standar Kepala Sekolah / Madrasah
  • Danielson FfT — when a domain anchors teaching practice (D2/D3)
  • UU 35/2014 · Permendikbud 46/2023 PPKSP · UNCRC — child protection / safeguarding layer (D5)

Coordinators open chips on the School Self-Appraisal page (AH) to verify a domain's framework anchor. This CH page is the network-wide review surface — per-domain chip inspection lives in the entry tool where the school self-rated.
